# Shadow Council v0.2.3 — focused human playtest

## Objective

Validate that both attacker and defender now have meaningful bluff decisions without making the game feel mathematical or hard to learn.

Use 4 human players where possible and play at least 3 consecutive matches.

## What changed

Attacker chooses:

- Threat 1–3
- hidden Force 0–Threat

Defender chooses a Defense Plan:

- Guard 0–3
- Challenge on/off
- Challenge costs 1 Power
- Guard and Challenge may be combined

Yield is Guard 0 + Challenge off and always costs 1 Influence.

## Observe

1. Does the attacker meaningfully vary Threat and Force?
2. Does the defender use all four defense styles, or does one dominate?
3. Does Guard + Challenge feel like useful insurance or an automatic best choice?
4. Does spending 1 Power to Challenge feel fair?
5. Does Yield remain attractive when Power is scarce?
6. Can players explain partial bluff after one game?
7. Do Schemes change who players target?
8. Is hidden Power producing reads rather than confusion?
9. Do players talk, bluff, accuse, or bargain naturally?
10. Are eliminations understandable from the reveal screen?

## Ask players after the session

- Did you feel you had enough control while defending?
- When you Challenged, did you understand exactly what you were betting?
- Did Guard + Challenge give you a useful middle ground?
- Did any option feel obviously correct most of the time?
- Did Threat sizes create psychological pressure or just arithmetic?
- Did you understand why you lost Influence after each reveal?
- Did the game feel deeper, or only more complicated?
- Did the interface feel like a game rather than a control panel?
- What moment was the most memorable?
- Would you immediately play another match?

## Stop conditions

Do not add new mechanics if any of these appear repeatedly:

- Guard + Challenge is the obvious answer to nearly every Strike.
- Challenge is rarely used because 1 Power is too expensive.
- Yield is never used.
- Threat 3 dominates all claims.
- Players still confuse Threat with confirmed damage.
- Defense planning takes too long.
- Scheme creates avoidance instead of interaction.
- Players cannot explain the reveal result.
- The game feels mathematical rather than social.

Tune or simplify the existing rules first.
